# ETL data files

The vocabularies the ETL maps Maetrim's DDOBuilderV2 files through. Each is embedded in the binary at compile time, so a change here needs a rebuild, and a value one of them cannot place fails the build on purpose.

- `effect_map.toml`: his item buff families and effect types, as stats, bonus types and effects.
- `legacy_drop_sources.toml`: the names in his drop text of quests that no longer exist, so the items only they dropped are flagged legacy (below).
- `source_aliases.toml`: the station or place names his drop text uses for a source recorded under another name: a crafting system's station, the place a challenge pack's rewards are turned in, or the place a vendor takes a turn-in (below).

## `effect_map.toml`

`damage_as_target` names stat-bonus Effect types whose `<Damage>` also supplies a stat target. EnergyResistance and SpellLore use it for the two Thunderforged augments. Other types keep their damage semantics. An unresolvable Damage target preserves the original targets and appears as `UNMAPPED DAMAGE TARGET` in the import report; the loader rejects declarations for types without a stat mapping.

`effects` is the single table for named effects, groups and stats. The seeded stat ids stay fixed; `is_stat = 1` rows have a `category`, `is_group = 1` rows name reusable groups, and all other effects are numbered after the stats. Names are unique across all kinds. A same-named effect granting only that stat is the stat row itself. Speed, Deception and Command are named effects that grant other stats. Attack Speed is split into Melee Attack Speed and Ranged Attack Speed. `effect_bonuses` targets a stat or a group; a group contains only stat members reading slot 1 and the link's type, with no nested groups. `owner_bonuses` expands group targets one level and retains `group_effect_id` on every expanded bonus. A direct link to a stat row already states its stat, value and bonus type. A direct stat link must carry a type and exactly one value slot. Its missing value is a WARN until corrected. Stat 135, Physical and Magical Resistance Rating, was retired; Sheltering now groups Physical Resistance Rating and Magical Resistance Rating.

Spell Focus Mastery is a named effect granting Spell DCs. Combat Mastery groups Trip, Sunder and Stun DCs and replaces Tactics, whose slug redirects to it. Assassinate is a named effect granting Assassinate DC. A source line with `Trip`, `Sunder`, `Stun` and `General` resolves the three distinct DCs once; `General` and `All` name those three stats. The Dolorous Invigorators retain separate Combat Mastery and Assassinate lines through cited `lines` corrections. Scion of the Astral Plane and the Reaper ring boost use the default grouped Tactical and Assassinate Effect. Baphomet’s Tactics composes Combat Mastery at a constant +13 Enhancement, with its damage proc retained as prose.

Potency is the group of each damage, healing and repair Spell Power stat, excluding Universal Spell Power, whose fully stacking contribution is separate. `[family.groups]` explicitly routes each merged wrapper's upstream buff type to its group, including `Potency = "Potency"`; it preserves the group's members independently of the buff's XML targets. Stat homes come from the explicit `STAT_HOME_TYPES` rules in `ddo-model`; named effects and groups keep their optional `home_bonus_type` in their own file. Enhancement, Equipment and Feat need no home declaration because enchantment lines always leave them unnamed. Source-backed `[[augment_combined_lines]]` entries join paired source rows into one line only when the recorded types and values still match.

When a buff definition has no usable display title, its effect name comes from the buff type split into words, without `Number`, `Numeral` or `Roman Numeral` markers. A skill group's description states its ability, amount and link bonus type.

`[family.item_named_effects]` routes an owner's `<Item>` token to a canonical named effect, before the normal buff definition route. Every matching canonical definition, including unused ranks, is loaded with its bonus rows and declaration before any owner is written in either XML build. A route with no canonical match fails; a missing owner rank fails with its buff and owner. Roman Riposte ranks keep direct constant rows and their proc prose, with composition declared for future factoring under #57. A slot-less rank takes no owner amounts. Numeric Riposte +N grants the full N to Armor Class and saves, with no proc, and stays distinct from Parrying through its cited declaration.

An `item_effect` correction with `field = "value2"` corrects the second slot without changing the first; a missing upstream slot is recorded as null. An `item_effect` correction with `field = "effect"` replaces a link in place. Its qualifier names the destination linked by our file, and `upstream` names his old effect. A missing old link becomes stale with `"no such bonus"`; an absent or ambiguous destination fails the data build. Replacement clears amounts the destination does not read. Definition-level `effect` corrections qualified by `stat "name"` also support `scale` and `rounding`, using the same targeting and staleness checks as constants and fixed types.

`[family]` maps item `<Buff>` types; its `owner_type_precedence` list records source-kind and definition/text disagreements where the owner type wins over the definition. `[effect.fixed]`, `[effect.targeted]`, `[effect.by_item]` and `[effect.by_item_default]` map their `<Effect>` types and effects on feats, augments, sets and options. `[effect.targeted]` maps exact target words to stat lists, such as `All` attack alacrity to both melee and ranged speed. A target list covering every bow, every crossbow and Dart is the ranged class; other thrown weapons are not required. A list covering every melee weapon type is the melee class. A shorter weapon list stays qualified text with its melee or ranged kind. `[effect.companion_targets]` drops a companion word only when a sibling target maps; an unknown target keeps the whole line as text. `[effect.energy_target_artifacts]` lists stats on which an energy qualifier contradicts the stat's scope. `[effect.engine_only]` names mechanics that do not block mapped siblings. `[names]` resolves family-name collisions, `[tier_groups]` gives ordered steps, and the alias sections normalize Maetrim's item, weapon, stat and bonus-type words. `[stat_name_aliases]` keeps older source names mapped to the renamed school Focus stats without changing their seed ids. A class, weapon, school, energy or alchemical target in the qualifier vocabulary keeps the effect as qualified text unless it contradicts the stat. His other targets are treated as copy-paste artifacts and map without a qualifier.

`[groups]` declares reusable stat lists; a group row is materialized only when an owner or effect grants it. `[skill_ability_groups]` records the curated skill-to-ability list from [DDO Wiki Skills](https://ddowiki.com/page/Skills), read 2026-10-05. `SkillBonusAbility` with an ability target links its matching skill group directly, carrying the owner's value and type; no same-meaning wrapper effect is written. Command grants Charisma Skills from slot 1 at the owner’s bonus type, plus a fixed Hide Penalty from slot 2. Mixed definitions preserve their fixed member types while other members read the link’s type. All Ability Scores and Sheltering are groups; Melee and Ranged Power and Doublestrike and Doubleshot are declared groups that appear only if the build finds a source granting them through one amount and type source. `/v1/effects` lists groups with `kind=group`, and `bonus=<group name>` or any member stat finds owners through the expanded view.

A named effect or one of its aliases may declare `omits_bonus_type = true` when the game’s complete title omits the type for every supported link type. The chosen template’s marker suppresses automatic prefixes; descriptions still state their types. Marked templates cannot contain `%b1`. Stats, groups and their aliases cannot carry the marker. The declaration is ours and survives imports.

The family stores its `verbose_name_template`, description template and optional defaults. Definition defaults belong to the buff definition: its own named effect retains them, while a buff routed to a stat group fills each missing owner-link amount from its definition. Explicit amounts, including zero, win. Groups never store defaults. The amount count is derived from `{1}` and `{2}` in the templates; a stat row takes one value. A named-effect link stores only what its owner states, may omit a trailing amount, and can have no first value when Maetrim omits it. The effective amount for each slot is `COALESCE(link value, effect default)`. An unresolved slot contributes no bonus and renders empty. `effect_links_missing_first_amount` lists named-effect links with no first value or default. A text-only effect can carry values; `effects_text_only_with_item_values` lists those candidates for a stat decision. A static bonus uses `amount_from = 0` and its final `constant`; a slot-reading bonus may use positive `scale` with `down`, `up` or `nearest` rounding. `owner_bonuses` is the shared view over every owner link and stat rule. It omits resolved zero amounts because zero grants no bonus; `effect_links_with_only_zero_bonuses` lists those owner links for review. Its `amount_source` is `owner`, `default` or `constant`; `scale` records a derived amount's multiplier. A stat's bonus type comes from its link. A named effect's `%b1` templates take the link type; without `%b1`, a real fixed `<Bonus>` in the definition wins over the owner's default `<BonusType>`. An owner supplies the type when the definition says `Not Set` or source kinds define different types for the same stat, such as Illusion Save and Lay on Hands Uses. Each owner keeps its own type, never a union of other owners' types.

`[stat_type_effects]` routes an unaliased direct stat link at a declared type to its named effect on items, augments and socket options. It runs after grouping, line corrections and alias corrections, preserving the link position, type and amounts. Set tiers and feats retain their stat lines. Each target must grant exactly that stat through slot one, unscaled and unconditional, with the same home type; an always-unnamed type uses the shared omission rule instead of a redundant home declaration. The XML build validates the definitions supplied by C, and the HARD `stat_links_at_routed_types` check uses the writer’s same link predicate.

An API effect line carries `name`, the concise effect label, and `verbose_name`, its rendered line with the displayed type and first amount where that effect displays them. Its `value` also carries a fixed bonus constant when the owner link has no number. The description renders separately from the description template; an untitled paragraph becomes the description and uses the effect name as its line. The line omits its home bonus type, while the `bonus_type` field and description retain that type. The data and XML builds derive stat homes from the same typed Rust rules. The import reports item majorities for new stats and disagreements with those rules under **Stat home types**, without creating a home rule or recreating a stat file C deleted. Named effects and groups read their home from C, including its absence; only new upstream effects infer a home from a uniform fixed type or an untied item majority. A declaration of an always-unnamed type fails the data build. The lint checks that every stat home is known and hides a served line, and that every skill with a Competence carrier has its explicit rule. `[line_templates]` and `[description_templates]` record per-effect line and prose formats; `description_trim_period` removes a trailing period from source prose where the game tooltip omits it, preserving that source's bonus-type rule; `[definition_defaults]` records a shared definition amount, and `[fixed_buff_values]` records a number encoded in a source buff type so variants can link one effect family. The owner's explicit value wins over the buff-type number. A line normally signs its first amount. Bonus amounts in the description use the same signed formatter, while durations, difficulty classes, percentages and other non-bonus numbers keep their source format. Set-tier structured links render as game-style bonus sentences, with their units supplied by `[set_bonus_line_templates]` when the default sentence is insufficient. Insight appears as “Insightful” in the line. The golden wiki samples and their known copy or structure differences live in `xtask/data/tooltip_golden.json` and are checked by `cargo xtask wiki-tooltips --db PATH`.

Items, augments, feats, augment-slot options and set tiers all use the same effect identity and cached writer. One upstream multi-stat `<Effect>` becomes one grouped link for set tiers, augments, feats and socket options. The shared `groupings.rs` index reads corrected definitions from the canonical data build: a unique compatible grouping takes the link's single value and type; an absent stat set uses a declared `[groups]` entry or a new name in canonical stat order. Generated templates take each member stat's unit from its line template; groups with mixed units repeat the amount for each stat. Multiple candidates, partially mapped types, mixed amounts or types, two-slot definitions and incompatible fixed types keep the previous links and appear in the held-effect report. The declared-group set-tier collapse remains only for this temporary fallback. Six ability bonuses still use `All Ability Scores`. Item links continue to come from their individual `<Buff>`s. `effect_tier_groups` groups plain effect steps. A set tier can carry prose and structured lines when they state separate facts. `triggers` names events from Maetrim's “On …” display prefixes, separately from future state conditions. `effect_damage` holds typed dice on a trigger: a stated range from minimum to maximum is 1d(max−min+1)+(min−1), and `amount_from` 1 or 2 multiplies the roll by that link slot and `scale`. A nullable `modifiers.effect_id` records which effect a raw modifier resolves to. `effects_with_dice_but_no_damage_rows` groups dice modifiers lacking damage rows, including those with no resolved effect, by source kind and effect type; dice backfill remains a later data pass.

A wiki-backed owner exception uses a bonus correction with `field = "lines"`. Its `upstream` lists stat sets, one per upstream line: `[["Trip DC", "Sunder DC", "Stun DC", "Assassinate DC"]]` splits one line, while `[["Search"], ["Spot"]]` merges two. The qualifier names the union in canonical stat order, joined with commas, followed by ` / <bonus type>` and optionally ` / <value>` to distinguish equal stat sets at different amounts. Set-tier and socket-option prefixes retain their usual form. The owner's file holds the corrected links. Each upstream name must be a stat, and the file's lines must partition the union exactly, without overlaps. Splits match the derived stats before grouping lookup, so no upstream-only group definition is needed. The finished owner list supplies merge candidates by their expanded stats and resolved type. The writer checks the target's slot values and replaces the source links at their first position. A named split output keeps its effect identity even when it grants one stat; each named output must accept the upstream value and type. Splits are supported on augments, feats, socket options and set tiers; merges also support items. Item splits remain unsupported because individual item buffs do not use the split writer. A missing member reports `"no such bonus"`; changed amounts keep the merge when the members reading each slot still agree. An explicit merge keeps its named target rather than applying an upstream line alias. A split and a merge cannot both consume one line. The `feat_bonus` kind supports both `lines` directions, plus `value`, `bonus_type`, `add`, `remove` and `alias`. Scion of the Feywild adds its missing Force Spell Power before merging it with Sonic Spell Power. Shield Deflection uses `bonus_type` to correct all four shield-specific absorption amounts to Competence. Plague Wolf removes Intelligence before grouping and then corrects the remaining Charisma stat. Gnomish Proficiencies uses a `lines` correction to retain separate Haggle and Use Magic Device lines. `socket_option_bonus` supports both `lines` directions, `value`, `remove` and `alias`, with the correction on the owning item and the qualifier `socket "<label>", option "<name>", <effect> / <bonus type>`. Both selectors are required. An ambiguous pair fails with the owner file and every matching socket and option order. Missing options or members rebase as `"no such bonus"`. Ring of Lies uses it to select its upgrade option's Charisma Skills alias.

A two-slot `lines` merge fills each slot from the source links granting that slot’s stats. Every slot must receive a value, and all members reading one slot must agree; movement speed and attack speed can therefore retain different amounts on one Speed link. The XML writer checks the original inputs, while the data build checks the recorded partitions, target union, amounts and types. A `lines` merge may also name a constant-valued effect. Its source links must match every target stat, type and constant; the merged link carries no values. Value and type corrections apply to the finished source list before bonus additions, and additions append before merges. An existing stat or grouping at the added type is never duplicated: its matching amount resolves the add, while a different amount reports a conflict and leaves the affected merge stale. An add cannot feed a split on the same owner and type. Other addition kinds keep their existing order. A value correction behind an existing grouping remains invalid. A slot-valued rank keeps its link value: Parrying I uses 1, while Roman Riposte ranks use their constant rows. Rank representation is tracked by ddo-data#57.

An item's `description` correction can name one socket option with `qualifier = 'socket "<label>", option "<name>"'`. It stores the full upstream option description and uses the same unique-pair lookup as socket-option bonus corrections. A missing option is stale with `"no such bonus"`. The API serves this option prose separately from its effect lines, whose descriptions come from the linked effects.

`[[set_tier_prose_bonuses]]` maps exact upstream descriptions to a stat or grouping, type and value, only when the buff yields no structured link. An entry with neither `bonus_type` nor `value` links an existing text-only effect that has no amount slots or bonuses. Both fields must be present together for a valued entry. Conditional, scaling and unresolved descriptions remain prose. An import reports a vocabulary description that matched A but stopped matching B. Data builds reject valued prose that duplicates a stat or grouping name. `set_bonus_line_template` keeps named enchantments' set lines explicit about their stats; fixed-type definitions name that type, while link-typed definitions use `%b1`.

The check-db WARN `effects_sharing_a_stat_set` reports unresolved pairs sharing an expanded stat set through the same index. Wrappers and Roman-numeral ladders classify the work list without choosing winners. A verified effect carries `stat_set_verdict` (`distinct` or `composes`), a nonblank `stat_set_reason`, and a citation. Declared effects stay available by name but leave the default grouping candidates and unresolved pair report. The import preserves these declarations from the shared grouping index; `distinct` effects keep their flat bonus rows instead of factoring through another group. Void Lore is the merged Negative and Poison Spell Lore group; its Roman VI step remains distinct under ddo-data#57. Constants, scaled amounts and non-default rounding are outside the unresolved domain. The compose inventory still includes exact matches with constants and declarations. The pair check reports candidates for source verification; it does not fail the build. New groups live in `data/effects/stat-groups/`; the pre-existing `[groups]` vocabulary still handles bonus-row factoring.

The template WARNs share one tokenizer. `effect_templates_without_their_placeholders` checks amount literals against slots or constants, and lists a literal on a line with no bonus rows under T7. Its fixed-type check uses the served hidden-type rule: Enhancement, Equipment and the effect's home type count as stated. `effect_templates_with_digits_without_amounts` lists other number tokens in non-stat line templates when neither the line nor description contains `{1}`. It separates rank-named steps from other mechanic numbers, and effects with constants from those with no modelled amount. A no-bonus line already listed under T7 is omitted from this second list; a constant-valued line can have a wrong literal and a separate rank digit. Rank-named steps remain work for ddo-data#56.

Effect aliases are optional `[[alias]]` rows on an effect, group or stat, with a name and `verbose_name_template`. Each owner link stores its choice as `alias = "name"`; the build checks that the linked effect owns that alias and reports the owner file on failure. The XML writer chooses an alias when the original buff family name matches one on its routed effect. An `alias` correction on `item_bonus`, `augment_bonus`, `feat_bonus`, `socket_option_bonus` or `set_tier_bonus` supplies wiki-only wording, with upstream null. Alias names and slugs are unique across the effect namespace; an alias using its own member stat name is display-only, with no slug or search key. Aliases change the line name and template while retaining the canonical effect description and bonuses. Renaming or removing an alias requires updating its links in the same change.

The map loader rejects unknown stats and aliases, conflicting mappings, invalid templates, and repeated tier ranks. The writer fails when a mapped bonus lacks a type, naming its owner, effect and stat; a cited `bonus_type` correction can supply the missing type. Corrections for item, augment and set bonuses change links or stat rules through the same cached writer, and `effect_damage` corrections add or remove cited dice rows. An `effect` correction qualified by `stat "name"` changes that definition row’s `constant` or fixed `bonus_type`, or removes its member row. A removal records his constant amount or the existing typed bonus row as `upstream`; a known stat absent upstream becomes stale with `no such bonus`, while an unknown stat or malformed qualifier fails. Constant and type corrections require exactly one target; changed upstream values make corrections stale. A data file carrying a removal must omit that member. Definition corrections cannot change a grouping already selected by the writer. The build preserves wiki-created entries only until Maetrim carries the same corrected name; his row then wins and the wiki entry is removed.

## Items the build leaves out or flags

His `Items/` files carry some items that a player cannot get or that carry nothing a build uses. The build writes none of an item whose slots are all cosmetic, nor of a cosmetic shield (his `Cosmetic Shield` weapon type in the off-hand, which has no shield bonus, armour or effect), and records each in `excluded_items` with its reason (`cosmetic-only slots` or `cosmetic shield`), so a consumer can tell an item left out on purpose from one that is missing. A wiki vendor or event that lists an excluded item fails the build, naming the reason, so the listing drops it.

An item that once dropped but no longer does is written in full and flagged instead: `items.is_legacy = 1`. The build flags an item when its `DropLocation` has at least one segment (the text between `;` or line breaks) and every segment names a text in `legacy_drop_sources.toml` and no quest, quest chain, saga or adventure pack of his or of a wiki file. An item with any other segment is current, and its legacy segments simply link nothing. A legacy text that later becomes a quest's name again (a quest correction renaming one, say) is a current quest in every segment that names it.

Two other kinds of legacy item are flagged the same way. An item whose name carries `(legacy)` or `(historic)` as a parenthesised word, ignoring case, is an old version his files keep beside the current one (`Brawling Gloves (legacy) (level 12)`, `Allegiance (historic)`), whether it comes from his files or a wiki items file. An old version the wiki says was replaced and no longer drops, under a name that does not say so, is flagged by an `is_legacy` correction (`from = 0`, `to = 1`) citing the page; they are recorded as `[[ddobuilder_correction]]` notes in the item's data file. `/v1/items` leaves every legacy item out of its rows and `total` unless `include_legacy=true`, `/v1/items/{id}` serves one with `is_legacy: true`, `/v1/version` counts them as `legacy_items`, and the build report as `legacy_item_count`.

## `legacy_drop_sources.toml`

The case the file was made for: Update 56 retired the quests Temple of Elemental Evil Part One and Part Two and replaced them with the Temple of Elemental Evil area quests, whose loot tables differ. His files no longer carry the two quests, but 553 of his items still say `Temple of Elemental Evil Part One, and Temple of Elemental Evil Part Two any chest`, `Temple of Elemental Evil Part Two, Elemental nodes chests` and the like, and those are the items flagged.

```toml
[[legacy]]
text = "Temple of Elemental Evil Part One"
reason = "The quest was retired in Update 56, replaced by the Temple of Elemental Evil area quests, which have their own loot tables."
source = "https://ddowiki.com/page/Temple_of_Elemental_Evil_Part_One"
read = "2026-10-02"
```

- `text`: the name as his segments spell it. It is matched as quest names are: longest first, as whole words, ignoring the case of every letter after the first.
- `reason`: why the source no longer drops loot, in one sentence.
- `source`: the `https://` URL that shows it.
- `read`: the date the source was read, `YYYY-MM-DD`.

A name his text uses for a source that still exists under another name is not a legacy source. Animus says `Devil Assault (quest), End Chest`, while his quests are `Devil Assault (Normal)`, `(Hard)` and `(Elite)`: the segment links to the pack Devil Assault, and the wiki loot file links the item to the three quests.

The build fails, naming the entry, when the file has a field other than these, `text` or `reason` is empty, `source` is not an `https://` URL, `read` is not a real date, or the same `text` appears twice ignoring case. The XML reader reports the flagged items as `legacy_source_flagged_count`.

## `source_aliases.toml`

His drop text often names the place an item is made rather than the crafting system that makes it: `Magma Forge, Crafted from various ingredients` is Thunder-Forged crafting, and `Lahar, Turn in Nebula Fragment` is Nebula Fragment Crafting. A segment that names a wiki crafting system (`crafting_systems.name`, as whole words, ignoring the case of every letter after the first, longest first) links the item or augment to it as a `crafting_system` source (`Catalyst Crafting, Turn in ...`, `Viktranium Experiment crafting, Turn in ...`); this file maps the other names to a system, matched against the segment's head, the text before its first comma, parenthesis or colon, ignoring case.

```toml
[[crafting_system]]
text = "Magma Forge"
system = "Thunder-Forged"
reason = "The Magma Forge in the Ruins of Thunderholme is where Thunder-Forged items are crafted."
```

- `text`: the head as his segments spell it.
- `system`: a crafting system's name in the data files; an alias whose system the data files lack links nothing.
- `reason`: why the name stands for that system, in one sentence.

The build fails, naming the entry, when the file has a field or table other than these, a field is blank, or the same `text` appears twice ignoring case (with the same `contains`, for a vendor alias). A test checks that every `system` names a crafting system in the canonical data files. The XML reader counts the rows written as `drop_text_crafting_system_source_count`.

Every crafting series his drop text names is a crafting system in the data files, so each such segment links its loot; the "Restored" Dragoncraft, Elfcraft and Giantcraft armours (`... Turn in 10 Restored Dragon Relics` and the Elven and Giant equivalents) link to their system by that head, though the wiki has no recipe page for them.

A `[[challenge]]` entry (`text`, `pack`, `reason`) does the same for a challenge pack, an adventure pack of Maetrim's `Challenges.xml`: a segment whose head is its `text` links the item or augment as a `challenge` source of that pack, the reward its challenges' ingredients or commendations buy. His text names no challenge, only the place: `Vaults of the Artificers, Turn in various challenge ingredients` (the House Cannith challenges, whose pack in his files is Secrets of the Artificers; nine of them are in Free to Play, which the link does not name) and `Eveningstar, Turn in 5 Commendations: ...` (Eveningstar Challenge Pack). The build report counts `drop_text_challenge_source_count`, and lists in `unresolved_source_aliases` the texts of every alias whose system or pack the build lacks.

A `[[vendor]]` entry (`text`, an optional `contains`, `vendor`, `reason`) links the item or augment as a `vendor` source of a wiki vendor (a vendor file in the data files) when the segment's head is its `text` and, if `contains` is given, the segment holds that text too, ignoring case. `contains` tells apart two vendors at one place: `Blue Water Inn, Turn in 40 Vistani Talismans` is Raam Lukresh's and `Blue Water Inn, Turn in 60 Vistani Totems` Osah Lukresh's, and `Necropolis, Turn in Dark Scarab Powder` is Squire Rale's while other Necropolis heads are not. Two entries may share a `text` only with different `contains`. A segment that names a wiki vendor itself (`Necropolis, Turn in Eerie Scarab Powder to Squire Rale`) needs no alias. The build report counts `drop_text_vendor_source_count`, a test checks that every `vendor` names a wiki vendor, and `unresolved_source_aliases` lists the texts of the ones the build lacks.

No alias is needed for an iconic hero's starter gear: a segment whose head is `Advance to level N` links the item as a `starter` source with `character_level` N (his text gives only `Advance to level 15, End reward`), counted as `drop_text_starter_source_count`. The level is the only fact his text gives, so the kind needs no table of its own.

A `[[crafting_system]]` entry may also carry `min_minimum_level` and `max_minimum_level` (either or both, inclusive): it then links only loot whose minimum level falls in that range, so one station can stand for two systems. `Altar of Fecundity, Manufactured Ingredient Recipes` (93 items) is the case: the Altar makes the blanks of both Green Steel items (minimum level 11 or 12, so `max_minimum_level = 20`, 45 items) and Legendary Green Steel items (minimum level 26, so `min_minimum_level = 21`, 48 items). Two entries may share a `text` only when their level ranges do not overlap, and the build fails on a `min_minimum_level` above the `max_minimum_level`.
